MY DREAM SCHOOL: UMIKAZE GAKUEN

Read these notes aloud while showing the matching chapters.
This is an imagined school, not a real institution.

01. A school worth dreaming of.
Hello everyone! Today I want to show you my dream school. It is called Umikaze, which means sea breeze. It is not a real school. I designed it to be a place where students feel safe, curious and excited to learn. Let me take you inside.

02. More sea air. Less city noise.
My school would be near Kamakura in Japan. I chose this region because I love the sea and Japanese architecture. Students could come by train, walk or cycle. Being near the ocean would also mean learning how to respect nature and stay safe during emergencies.

03. Grow at your own pace.
I would create my own eleven-grade system. Sakura is for grades one to four, Nami is for grades five to eight, and Sora is for grades nine to eleven. Younger children would learn through play and projects. Older students would choose more subjects. We would have three terms, with breaks between them. This is my dream system, not the official Japanese one.

04. Light, wood and open minds.
The building would be modern, but it would still have a Japanese atmosphere. I would use light wood, white walls and large windows. The school would have a lift and step-free routes. Solar panels and a garden would help students learn about sustainability in a practical way.

05. A welcome, not a warning.
This is the entrance hall. Students would change their shoes here, which helps keep the school clean. I would put comfortable benches and plants in the corridors. I also like the idea of students helping to tidy their classrooms. The most important rule would be simple: treat people kindly.

06. Curiosity gets a seat.
In my classroom, there would be no more than twenty students. Lessons would begin at nine, so we would not have to wake up extremely early. We would use technology, but we would also read books, write by hand and work together. I would give less homework and more meaningful projects.

07. Teachers who know your name.
My ideal teachers would be friendly and fair. They would explain things in different ways and encourage us to ask questions. Every student would have a mentor. If someone felt stressed or had a problem, they could talk to a counsellor without feeling embarrassed.

08. Good food. Better company.
The canteen is one of my favourite parts. Students could choose ramen, curry, vegetable rice bowls or a bento lunch. The food would be included, so nobody would miss lunch because of money. We would have enough time to eat, talk and relax before the next lesson.

09. Get lost in a good book.
This is my dream library. I would fill it with books in Japanese and English, including manga. There would be window seats and a reading tree in the middle. It would be a quiet place where students could read, recharge or discover a completely new interest.

10. Not every talent fits a textbook.
I would offer different clubs because everyone has different talents. Some students could play music or draw manga. Others could build robots, learn calligraphy or explore tea culture. Clubs would be optional, and students could change clubs if they wanted to try something new.

11. Play for joy. Not just medals.
Sport would not only be for the best athletes. Everyone could find an activity they enjoy. My school would have a pool, a field and a running track. I would also love a surf club, but only with qualified instructors and safe weather. The aim would be health and confidence.

12. A garden above. A future ahead.
Finally, this is the rooftop garden. Students could grow plants, sit with friends and learn about solar energy. In the evening, the astronomy club could use a telescope. The roof would only open with supervision and proper safety barriers.

13. A school I would love to go to.
So, this is Umikaze, my dream school. I chose a beautiful location, but the most important part is how people would feel here. I want a school where students feel supported and excited to learn. Thank you for listening! Would you like to study at Umikaze?
